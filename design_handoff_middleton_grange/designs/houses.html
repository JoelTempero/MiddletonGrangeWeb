A note before we begin

To name a House is to choose what we want our children to become.

Every student at Middleton belongs to one of four Houses. They run for them, they sing for them, they cheer for them on athletics day and they sit beside House-mates from other year levels they would otherwise never meet.

Three of our Houses are named for the men who left from Lyttelton, ninety minutes' drive over the hill, bound for the ice. They knew what they were going into. They went anyway. The fourth — Bowen — is named for the family on whose Upper Riccarton paddocks our first classrooms were built in 1964.

To descend the page is to descend the latitude. Each House appears where its namesake was bravest.

ii.House — Scott · the Terra Nova expedition

Scott.

1868 — 1912 Captain Robert Falcon Scott, Royal Navy. Led the Terra Nova to the South Pole in 1912. Reached it on the 17th of January. Did not return. Eleven miles short of the next supply depot.
House № I77°40′ S
S
Terra Nova1910 – 1913
For God's sake, look after our people.
Scott's last journal entry · March 1912

Scott reminds us what it costs to attempt great things. He lost. He was beaten to the Pole by Amundsen by thirty-three days. The return journey killed him and his four companions.

But he wrote home, in pencil, in a tent in a blizzard, until the words ran out. The patron, in our House system, of finishing what you start — and of doing it with grace when the wind has turned against you.

iii.House — Wilson · the naturalist

Wilson.

1872 — 1912 Dr Edward Adrian Wilson. Naturalist, surgeon, watercolourist. Chief scientist of the Terra Nova. Scott's closest friend. Walked beside him to the Pole, and back, and died in the same tent.
House № III77°50′ S
W
Polar PartyNaturalist
All the noblest things in life require us to live in the present.
Edward Wilson · letters from the ice

Wilson is the patron of curiosity. Of the field notebook, the patient eye, the watercolour painted in mittens at minus forty. He brought home the world's first emperor penguin eggs from a winter march that even his companions called madness.

For Wilson House, the lesson is this: to look closely. To draw what you see. To love a small thing — a moss, a feather, an algebra problem — well enough that you would walk into a blizzard for it.

iv.House — Shackleton · who brought every man home

Shackleton.

1874 — 1922 Sir Ernest Henry Shackleton. Turned back ninety-seven miles from the Pole because his men were starving. Years later, lost the Endurance to the ice in the Weddell Sea — and brought every member of his crew home alive. Every one.
House № II88°23′ S
E
Endurance1914 – 1917
By endurance we conquer.
Shackleton family motto · ship's nameplate

Shackleton, on his furthest-south march in 1909, looked at the wind and the rations and the bones of his men and decided that a live donkey is worth more than a dead lion. He turned around. The Pole would wait.

The patron of resilience, but more than that — of leadership that brings the team home. Of the captain who eats last. Of the unglamorous decision that nobody puts on the front page. The Endurance house, for the storms our students will weather long after they leave us.
