About — the school in brief

About us.

Middleton Grange is a state-integrated Christian school in Upper Riccarton, Ōtautahi Christchurch. We teach about fourteen hundred young people, from Year 1 to Year 13, on one campus, with one verse.

A letter, in full

Thank you for taking a moment to visit us. Choosing a school for your child is one of the most significant decisions you will ever make, and I do not take lightly that you are considering Middleton Grange.

We are a school known for its high quality education and, equally, as a compassionate community in which the God-given gifts and talents of students are noticed, nurtured and celebrated. Those three words — noticed, nurtured, celebrated — matter to us in that order.

From Year 1 to Year 13, on one campus, your child will be known by name. They will be challenged academically — our seniors consistently exceed the national NCEA averages — but more importantly they will be shaped, slowly, into the kind of person who notices the new kid, who finishes what they started, who carries themselves with quiet confidence and quiet humility both. That is character. That is what we are here to do.

If you would like to see the school in action, the best invitation I can offer is our Open Day in May, or a quiet weekday tour. Walk the campus. Sit in on a chapel. Eat something terrible from the canteen. You will get a far better sense of who we are in twenty minutes here than in a hundred pages of brochure.

I look forward, perhaps, to meeting you.

— Gregg Le Roux
Principal · Middleton Grange School
Our story — 1964 to today

Sixty-two years of one school, on one campus.

Middleton Grange opened on the 4th of February, 1964, on what had then been farmland in Upper Riccarton. We have not moved since. What follows is the short version.

Decade I
1964

The school begins.

Founded by The Christian Schools' Trust on Bowen farmland, Upper Riccarton. Twenty-three students. A handful of converted classrooms.

Decade II
1970

The secondary years.

The school grows beyond primary, becoming a full Year 1 – 13. The chapel and original gymnasium are built. The four-Houses system is established.

Decade III
1980

A community takes root.

The roll passes 500. Performing arts begins in earnest. Sport codes broaden from rugby and netball to nearly everything.

Decade IV
1996

State integration.

Middleton joins the New Zealand state-integrated system, preserving its special Christian character while accessing government funding. The Christian Schools' Trust remains proprietor.

Decade V
2010

The Quake.

The school weathers the Canterbury earthquakes. Classes continue. Buildings are strengthened and renewed. International College grows.

Decade VI
2020

The Grange Theatre opens.

Three-hundred-and-four seats. Built for our productions and for the wider Christchurch community to use. The Fridge Radio & Te Ohu Kahika launch.

Today
2026

Sixty-two years on.

Around fourteen hundred students. Four schools. One verse. The 60th-anniversary programme runs through Term 3 with a gala in September.

Special Character

A Christian school, state-integrated, non-denominational, sixty-two years old.

As a Christian school, the Bible is the foundation for all we do. We believe that education is fundamentally about preparing young people to live lives that honour God and serve others.

Our Special Character is not an add-on; it is the soil. It is in how we choose subjects, how we run teams, how we write reports, how we welcome a new family through the gates. It shapes the Year 4 Bible lesson, the Year 12 NCEA history class, the way we coach the 1st XV, the way we run the school production.

Middleton is interdenominational by design. Our families come from Baptist, Anglican, Catholic, Presbyterian, Pentecostal, Reformed, Brethren and many other traditions. They are bound together by a shared belief: that Christ is at the centre, and that every child is made in the image of God and is worth the effort of being noticed, nurtured and celebrated.

Life at MGS · Four Schools, one community

One school, four ways in.

i.
Primary Years 1 – 6 · ~300 ākonga

Foundations are quiet, slow work. Reading taught like it matters because it does. The children play in puddles, learn each other's names, and discover what they are good at.

Walk through ↗
ii.
Middle Years 7 – 10 · ~450 ākonga

The middle years are when questions get harder and answers more interesting. Real responsibility, real failure, real friendship — and a great deal of patience from the adults in the room.

Walk through ↗
iii.
Senior CollegeYears 11 – 13 · ~550 ākonga

NCEA, leadership, service, university and trades. We aim to send our seniors out the gate at the end of Year 13 well-loved and well-prepared — and only one of those is something we'll put on a brochure.

iv.
InternationalYears 1 – 13 · ~100 ākonga · 14 countries

For our students from overseas — homestay families, ESOL, pastoral care, university pathways, and a tutor who will text your parents in their own time zone if you are sick.
